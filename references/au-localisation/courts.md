## Federal courts

### High Court of Australia
- **Role**: Final court of appeal for all Australian courts. Original jurisdiction over constitutional matters and matters under s 75 of the Constitution.
- **Access**: Special leave required for most appeals.
- **Seat**: Canberra; sits in capital cities periodically.

### Federal Court of Australia
- **Jurisdiction**: Conferred by Commonwealth statute. Includes: corporations law, competition law (ACCC matters), consumer law, intellectual property, taxation, bankruptcy (with FCFCOA), industrial relations, native title, admiralty, migration (limited), human rights complaints.
- **Rules**: Federal Court Rules 2011 (Cth). Discovery is by category, not automatic; case management strong (Practice Notes).
- **Appeals**: Single judge to Full Court (3 judges) of the Federal Court. Then by special leave to the High Court.

### Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia (FCFCOA)
- **Division 1**: Family law and child support (most), some general federal law.
- **Division 2**: Family law, bankruptcy, migration, general federal law, fair work small claims (under $100,000), human rights.
- **Created**: 1 September 2021, merging the Family Court of Australia and the Federal Circuit Court.

## State and territory courts

Each state and territory has a three-tier (or two-tier in smaller jurisdictions) court structure. Subject-matter and quantum thresholds vary.

| State/Territory | Supreme Court (unlimited) | Intermediate (mid-tier civil + indictable criminal) | Lower (small civil + summary criminal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| NSW | Supreme Court | District Court | Local Court |
| VIC | Supreme Court | County Court | Magistrates' Court |
| QLD | Supreme Court | District Court | Magistrates Court |
| WA | Supreme Court | District Court | Magistrates Court |
| SA | Supreme Court | District Court | Magistrates Court |
| TAS | Supreme Court | (none, two-tier) | Magistrates Court |
| ACT | Supreme Court | (none, two-tier) | Magistrates Court |
| NT | Supreme Court | (none, two-tier) | Local Court |

State Supreme Courts have Courts of Appeal (or Court of Criminal Appeal in some) sitting on appeals from single judges of the same court and from intermediate courts.

### Procedural rules (state)
- NSW: Uniform Civil Procedure Rules 2005 (NSW), Civil Procedure Act 2005 (NSW)
- VIC: Supreme Court (General Civil Procedure) Rules 2015, Civil Procedure Act 2010 (VIC)
- QLD: Uniform Civil Procedure Rules 1999 (QLD)
- WA: Rules of the Supreme Court 1971 (WA), Civil Judgments Enforcement Act 2004 (WA)
- SA: Uniform Civil Rules 2020 (SA)
- TAS: Supreme Court Rules 2000 (TAS)
- ACT: Court Procedures Rules 2006 (ACT)
- NT: Supreme Court Rules 1987 (NT)

## Tribunals

Tribunals do significant work in Australia. They are not bound by the rules of evidence and apply more flexible procedure. Key tribunals:

### Commonwealth
- **Administrative Review Tribunal (ART)**: Merits review of most Commonwealth administrative decisions. Replaced the Administrative Appeals Tribunal (AAT) on 14 October 2024. Divisions include General, Migration and Refugee, Social Security, Taxation and Business, Veterans' and Workers' Compensation.
- **Fair Work Commission (FWC)**: Unfair dismissal, general protections, sexual harassment and bullying jurisdictions, modern awards, enterprise agreements, industrial action.
- **Copyright Tribunal**: Royalty disputes under the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth).
- **Trade Marks Office hearings (IP Australia)**: Opposition and removal proceedings administered by IP Australia (not a court, but a tribunal-like process).

### State
- **NSW**: NCAT (Civil and Administrative Tribunal of NSW). Consumer claims, residential tenancies, administrative review, occupational regulation, guardianship.
- **VIC**: VCAT (Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal).
- **QLD**: QCAT (Queensland Civil and Administrative Tribunal).
- **WA**: SAT (State Administrative Tribunal of WA).
- **SA**: SACAT (South Australian Civil and Administrative Tribunal).
- **TAS**: TASCAT (Tasmanian Civil and Administrative Tribunal, commenced 2021).
- **ACT**: ACAT (ACT Civil and Administrative Tribunal).
- **NT**: NTCAT (NT Civil and Administrative Tribunal).

## Procedural differences from the US

A non-exhaustive list of differences that catch out US-trained lawyers and US-default skills:

1. **Discovery is not automatic**. Australian courts require disclosure / discovery only by order. Federal Court typically orders disclosure of categories of documents, not all relevant documents.
2. **Depositions do not exist as a routine procedure**. Examination of witnesses pre-trial occurs only on special grounds (e.g. preservation of evidence). Cross-examination happens at hearing.
3. **Interrogatories are restricted**. Permitted in some jurisdictions but rarely used; leave usually required.
4. **Pleadings carry more weight**. The pleadings define the issues. Departure from pleadings at trial requires amendment.
5. **Costs follow the event**. The losing party generally pays a portion of the winning party's costs (party-party basis, around 60-70% of actual). This is structural; it changes incentives in ways US lawyers find surprising.
6. **No civil jury trials in most jurisdictions** (Victoria a notable exception for some matters; defamation in some states). Most civil matters are judge-alone.
7. **Litigation privilege requires the dominant purpose of use in existing or anticipated litigation**, not just a reasonable apprehension of litigation. See `privilege.md`.
8. **Settlement communications**: "without prejudice" (and "without prejudice save as to costs", or Calderbank offers) achieve roughly what FRE 408 achieves, but the mechanics and case law are distinct. Don't import US framing.
9. **Class actions**: Part IVA of the Federal Court of Australia Act 1976 (Cth) and state equivalents (VIC Part 4A SCA, NSW Part 10 CPA, QLD Part 13A CPA). Opt-out as default. Litigation funding is permitted and well-established. Common fund orders post-BMW v Brewster (2019) require specific statutory authority (now provided in Federal Court for closed classes).
10. **No DMCA-style safe harbour for general internet intermediaries**. Australia's Copyright Act 1968 (Cth) limited safe harbour was restricted to carriage service providers until 2018; extended to education, libraries, archives, cultural institutions, and disability organisations by the Copyright Amendment (Service Providers) Act 2018 (Cth). Not applicable to commercial UGC platforms.
11. **No discovery sanctions akin to FRCP 37 spoliation jurisprudence**. Document preservation obligations exist (e.g. in NSW under r 5.4 UCPR and Court's inherent jurisdiction; see *Palavi v Queensland Newspapers Pty Ltd* and the *British American Tobacco* line of cases) but the framework is different.

## Citation of court names in AGLC

- High Court of Australia: HCA in medium neutral citation (e.g. *Mabo v Queensland (No 2)* (1992) 175 CLR 1).
- Federal Court of Australia (single judge): FCA. Full Court: FCAFC.
- State Supreme Courts: state abbreviation + SC (e.g. *Smith v Jones* [2024] NSWSC 123). Courts of Appeal: state + CA (e.g. NSWCA, VSCA).
